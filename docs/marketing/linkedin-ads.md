## 3. Video critique: selling to a program director in the first 3 seconds

The video is clear and works muted. The problem is the order: it opens like a small-business ad and saves the strongest nonprofit proof (the real Impact PDF) for second 24, after most viewers have scrolled on.

- **Change the hook to the sticky note you already have.** "Still tracking clients on sticky notes?" could be any business. Open on "Funder report due Friday?" as the big caption, with the sticky notes behind it. A program director feels that one.
- **Replace the haircut example.** "Ana Ortiz – haircut $45" at second 4 tells a director this is salon software. For this cut, type something like "Rosa M. – resume help, bus pass" → "Check-in logged". Keep the haircut for the local-business cut.
- **Show the report second, not last.** Right after the hook (seconds 3 to 7), fan out the Impact PDF with "Funder-ready reports. One click." Then spend the rest showing how the data gets there: one-tap check-in, type-it command box, Team view, referrals.
- **Cut "Clients, notes, tasks, calendar and insights."** It's a feature list with no pain attached, and "insights" is the business word (organizations see "Impact"). You get 3 to 4 seconds back.
- **Put the name on screen from the first frame.** A small Surfdesk logo in a corner for the whole video. Right now a muted viewer who leaves at second 10 never learns who the ad was from.
- **Add the privacy beat to the Team view.** Change "Your team's whole day, at a glance." to two captions: "Your team's whole day, at a glance." then "Their personal tasks stay private." It answers the objection staff will raise.
- **Make the report shot say "your logo".** Add "Your logo. No Surfdesk branding." under the PDF pages. White-label is a real selling point for anything that goes to a funder.
- **Fix the end card and the shape.** For this audience: "Surfdesk. Know every person you serve. Start a 14-day free team trial at thesurfdesk.app", held for at least 3 seconds. Export square (1:1) or vertical (4:5) with large captions for phone feeds, and make a 15-second version (hook, report, check-in, end card) to test against the 29-second one.

## 4. Targeting and starter test plan

### Targeting (one audience, saved as "NP directors US")

- **Location:** United States. If you would rather start where you can also meet people in person, pick your state plus two or three neighboring ones, as long as the audience stays above roughly 30,000.
- **Job titles (any of):** Executive Director, Program Director, Director of Programs, Program Manager, Chief Program Officer, Director of Operations, Operations Manager, Chief Operating Officer, Director of Client Services, Workforce Development Director, Workforce Development Manager, Community Services Director, Case Management Supervisor, Reentry Program Manager, Family Resource Center Director.
- **AND company industries (any of):** Non-profit Organizations, Individual and Family Services, Civic and Social Organizations, Community Services, Vocational Rehabilitation Services. The industry filter matters: without it "Executive Director" pulls in banks. (Pick the closest names Campaign Manager offers; I wrote these from memory, not from a live check.)
- **AND company size:** 11–50 and 51–200 employees. Add 2–10 only if the audience is too small. Skip 1,000+; they buy through procurement.
- **Exclude:** job functions Sales and Business Development (vendors selling to nonprofits).
- **Turn off:** Audience Expansion and LinkedIn Audience Network. Both spend a small budget on people outside the list.
- **Target size:** 30,000 to 100,000. Smaller than that and the same people see the ad too often; bigger and the titles are too loose.

### Round 1: which angle wins (14 days, about $280)

- **Setup:** one campaign, objective Website visits, $20 a day, all three nonprofit variants (A, B, C) with the same video, ad rotation set to "Rotate ads evenly". One campaign instead of three keeps you at $20 a day instead of $30 (the minimum is $10 per campaign).
- **Split:** even, about a third each. Start Tuesday morning.
- **What decides the winner:** click-through rate on clicks to thesurfdesk.app (landing-page clicks, not total clicks, which also count "see more" and reactions). Tie-breaker: cost per landing-page click. At this budget there won't be enough sign-ups to judge by sign-ups, so treat them as a sanity check in the owner command center, not the scoreboard.
- **When to kill a variant:** once it has at least 1,500 impressions (or about $60 spent), pause it if its click-through rate is less than half the leader's. Don't judge anything before that; early numbers swing wildly.
- **When to call it:** day 14, or when each remaining ad passes 3,000 impressions. If two are within 20% of each other, call it a tie and keep both.
- **When to stop altogether:** if the $280 is spent and no team trial has started, pause the ads and put the time back into the personal emails in `outreach.md`. Expect LinkedIn clicks to cost several dollars each, so this test buys information, not volume.

### Round 2 (only if round 1 produced a trial or a clear winner)

- Winning copy with the current video vs. the recut video from section 3 (report-first hook). $20 a day, 14 days, same rules.
- Then test the button: "Learn more" vs. "Sign up".

### Local-business variant

Run it as its own campaign alongside round 1: $10 a day for 14 days (about $140), objective Website visits, same click-through rule.
- **Job titles:** Owner, Co-Owner, Founder, Practice Manager, Clinic Manager, Office Manager, Studio Owner, Salon Owner, General Manager.
- **AND industries (closest names in Campaign Manager; written from memory, confirm there):** Veterinary Services, Pet Services, Personal Care Services (salons, spas, barbers), Wellness and Fitness Services, Education (tutoring), Consumer Services.
- **Company size:** 1–10 and 11–50.
- **Location:** start with your metro area or state; local owners respond better to a local founder.
- Expect Instagram and Facebook to reach these owners more cheaply; use LinkedIn results to see which business type responds, then put more budget there.

### Before launch

- Sign-ups by source: check whether the owner command center can show where a sign-up came from. If not, compare sign-up counts on ad days against the week before.
- LinkedIn's Insight Tag (their tracking pixel) would give real conversion numbers, but it adds a new tracker to the site. If you install it, `/privacy` needs to name it and `LEGAL_VERSION` gets bumped. The UTM tags above need none of that.
- The workforce program that uses Surfdesk daily is your best proof. Once their director agrees to a one-line quote, put it in Variant A's intro. Until then, no customer names or quotes in ads.
